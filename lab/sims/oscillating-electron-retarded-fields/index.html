READ THE FIELD

Curves integrate the instantaneous B field in 3-D. Cyan/yellow = sign of its fixed +y component; arrowheads show the full vector. Gray oval = prescribed trajectory, not B. A (2,0,1) and B (5,0,1) are fixed observers. Brightness compresses strength. Encoding details ↓

RADIATION / SOURCE TIME

Power has no sign.

Turning mode reports the full-sphere Liénard power. The meridian plot is available only for the axisymmetric linear comparison: radius encodes dP/dΩ, normalized to its current maximum; dashed is sin²θ. Both use emission time t.

A MAP, NOT A MATERIAL

What the light means

These curves are integrated magnetic streamlines at the displayed observation time. Near the turning electron they bend differently from distant curves, whose source information is older. They are recomputed each frame: they are not emitted rings, photons or moving material.

Arrows follow the full B vector. Cyan/yellow records the sign of B’s +y component; color is not a topological fold claim. Gray is the prescribed path. White is the present electron and its velocity; amber and the dashed light path belong to the probe alone. No guide is presented as a computed field.

Brightness compresses |B| logarithmically; curve density is a drawing choice, not magnetic flux. Integration and finite seeding can miss fine structures. Numerical and rendering limits ↗

THE MODEL’S BOUNDARY

Prescribed classical motion

The external drive supplies the energy. Radiation reaction, quantum structure and the driving apparatus’s own fields are omitted. This is a single net charge, not a neutral dipole. The exact field includes velocity and acceleration terms; “radiation only” isolates the acceleration term.

Dimensionless length cT and time T; E unit |e|/(4πε₀c²T²), B unit E/c. The hairpin has semi-axes 1.4 and 3; its angular rate is β₀/3. The linear comparison has ωT = 1. Derivation, checks and limitations ↗ · Historical illustration ↗
